## The developer's reading (2026-10-27)
Each box shows a person and a thing, and the layer of English vocabulary it names determines how the two are related:

| Layer | What the diagram shows | What it means (developer's reading) |
|---|---|---|
| **Saxon-Nordic** | Wavy lines running across the person and the thing | A direct, personal, equality-based, human-Earth connection between the speaker and the thing |
| **Hispano-Frankish** | A blocky, brick-wall barrier between the person and the thing | A division: a strata separation between the person and the thing |
| **Latin** | Double-wavy dotted lines between the person and the thing | An approachment between the person and the thing |
| **Greek** | A dotted arrow from the figure to the thing | A psychological or cognitive understanding of the thing by the person |

## Fit with the canon note
Canon item C-7 already says English takes French for a higher register, Greek for mental contemplation and psychological understanding, and German for common core usage. The diagram appears to give the reasoning behind those assignments: it describes the kind of relationship the speaker has to the thing named, and register follows from that. This correspondence is my reading, to be confirmed by the developer. Latin does not appear in C-7, so its "approachment" is new information.

## Reading of the shapes (to confirm)
The developer explained the lines, not the shapes. In the photo each box has a circle and a square on the left (the person) and a stack of circles on the right (the thing). One reading that fits all four panels: the circle is the head or mind and the square is the body. Then Saxon-Nordic connects both, the whole person to the thing; Greek connects only the circle, the mind, while the square stays apart, which matches "cognitive understanding"; Hispano-Frankish puts a wall in the way; Latin draws both toward the thing. **This is my guess, not the developer's statement.**

## The principle behind it (developer, 2026-10-27; D-18 Source layers as ways of thinking)
A layer is not just a stock of words. It is **how a language uses different linguistic origins to approach different concepts**, and it reaches **grammar as well as vocabulary**. Each layer gives the language a different "way of thinking" about a particular topic or area.

**The developer's worked example, English possession.** The choice of possessive form follows the possessor:
- An animate possessor takes the "'s" form: *John's sister* (Saxon-Nordic structure), not "the sister of John."
- An inanimate possessor takes the "of" form: *the screen of the television* (Hispano-Frankish structure), not "the television's screen."

The developer's point is that these approaches **permeate every area of English**, not just its vocabulary. (My note: English allows both forms in many cases and the preference is a strong default, not an absolute rule; the developer's statement is the default tendency. RQ-B8 checks this against evidence on English genitive choice.)

**For Tepenian Hawaiian.** Each contributing source language causes the language to approach a particular topic or area in its own way, the way English does. The topics and areas Middle Hawaiian "thinks" about **will probably not line up one-to-one with English's**, though some may. So:
- The layering table's rows should not be copied from English's areas (formal register, mental contemplation, common core). They should come from the topics and areas where Tepenian Hawaiian's own sources actually pull it in different directions, found by studying the sources on their own terms. This agrees with the governing rule in the `slang-synthesis` diagram methodology: analyze each language on its own terms, not as English with differences (`supplementary_diagrams/grammar_logic_diagrams/00_Diagram_Notation_and_Methodology.md` in that repo).
- A layering row should be able to say both which source governs an area and what way of thinking it brings, including in grammar (for example how possession, definiteness or tense is handled there), not only which words come from where.

**An idea to investigate (developer, 2026-10-27; not a decision).** The animate/inanimate split in English possession may bear on the canon's questions about robots (property versus person). The developer's thought: Tepenian Hawaiian may have separate "areas" for three kinds of referent, **robots, humans, and actual inanimate objects**, each with its own way of thinking in grammar and vocabulary. See RQ-A6 Three-way animacy: robots, humans, objects and Q-13 Grammatical classing of robots, humans and objects.

**Caution (repo rule 6, no national stereotypes).** The diagram describes how layers of English vocabulary function, in the developer's account. It says nothing about the character of the people who speak the source languages, and a layering row for Tepenian Hawaiian must not be argued from the character of a people.
