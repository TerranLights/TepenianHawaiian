# Ideas for Further Investigation

A rough, unsorted collection of possibilities that may theoretically become part of the Hawaiian Pidgin, which has become its own language. 

These are not conclusive rules. They are strictly just ideas that are to be investigated via research into the specific involved in the synthesis of Tepenian Hawaiian, as well as investigation and research into the broader field of Linguistics as a whole.

### Unresearched Ideas

- the adjectival/noun suffix for a persion who is from a place (most prominently from a country):
    - if the placename-noun ends in either "N" or a vowel other than "A" or in "[Consonant]A", then the ending is [~]ji
    - if the placename-noun ends in a consonant, then the ending is [~]iji
    - if the placename-noun ends in "[~]ia", then the ending undergoes a phonetic shift: [~]ia --> [~]iji
        - e.g.: Canada/Canadaji, Mexico/Mexicoji, America/Americaji, Japan/Japanji, Cascadia/Cascadiaiji, Brazil/Braziliji, Kazakhstan/Kazakhstaniji, etc etc
- nouns would be able to use the Definite article ("The"), but *not* the Indefinite article ("a[n]") // i.e., it functions like Icelandic
- "San" and "Sama" are used similarly to the way we might use "Sir", "Madam", "Father" in English, or "Don/Doña", "Caballero", "Señor[a]" in Spanish, etc
    - "Excuse me, Sama, could you tell me the way to the library?"
    - "Pardon me, San, might I ask you to please move over to make some space? We're expecting a delivery."
- "Kun"/"Chan" are the male and female equivalent of "bro", "friend", "fella", "chum", etc etc
- nouns would not distinguish between Singular and Plural. Everything has one form, and quantity is shown via qualifiers
    - One chair. Two chair. Three chair.
    - One car. Two car. Three car.
    - One book. Two book. Three book.
- Nouns would be genderless. // (some possible niche exceptions might exist among Spanish-derivative vocabulary)
